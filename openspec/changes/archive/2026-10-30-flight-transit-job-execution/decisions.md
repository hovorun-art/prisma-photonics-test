# Decisions: flight-transit-job-execution

## Decided

- The change is planned and built on its own branch `flight-transit-job-execution` (user)
- Scope: generating the transit report in job-execution-service: flight, aircraft, times, great-circle path and waypoints (user, roadmap milestone 3)
- Carried over from `flight-transit-job`: the payload `{ origin, destination, departureDate }` names two different bundled airports and a calendar date; the time of day is generated by the worker; the report shape is `flight`, `aircraft { model, registration }`, `origin` / `destination { iata, name, city, timeZone, lat, lon }`, `departureAt`, `arrivalAt`, `distanceKm`, `durationMin`, `cruiseAltitudeFt`, `path` (`[lat, lon]` pairs, at least 2), `waypoints[] { name, at, lat, lon, altitudeFt, groundSpeedKmh, distanceFlownKm }` (at least 2); a failed attempt uses the shared `JobError` (user)
- Aircraft transit data is generated from the bundled airport list, no external API (user, concept)
- The flight leaves at a random time (whole 5 minutes) within the departure date in the origin airport's local time zone; `departureAt` is stored as UTC (user)
- Waypoints follow the flight phases: Departure (origin IATA), Top of climb, a configured number of evenly spaced cruise points named "Cruise 1..N" (4 by default), Top of descent, Arrival (destination IATA); each with time, position, altitude, ground speed and distance flown (user)
- Altitude and ground speed follow climb-cruise-descent: 0 ft and taxi speed at the airports, a linear climb to cruise altitude over a configured climb distance, constant cruise altitude and speed, a linear descent at the end; a flight shorter than climb plus descent peaks lower (user)
- Aircraft by distance class: narrow-body (A320neo, B737 MAX) under a configured distance (4000 km by default), wide-body (A350-900, B787-9, B777-300ER) above, each model with its own cruise speed and altitude; flight number is a random airline code from a small list plus 1-4 digits (e.g. `BA 117`); registration random in a common format (e.g. `G-XWBA`) (user)
- Each attempt generates a new report with fresh random values; only the completed attempt's report is stored (user)
- Running a transit job takes a configured wait and fails at random at a configured rate, like webhook, to exercise retries on this type too (user)
- The wait is a fixed configured time, 2000 ms by default (`FLIGHT_TRANSIT_GENERATE_MS`), through the `Sleeper` port; the failure is drawn once per attempt after the wait against a configured rate, 0.1 by default (`FLIGHT_TRANSIT_FAILURE_RATE`; 0 never fails, 1 always fails); defaults only in the config parser (user)
- A failed attempt stores code `FLIGHT_TRANSIT_DATA_UNAVAILABLE`, message "Flight data for <ORIGIN>-<DESTINATION> is unavailable", details `{ origin, destination }` (user)
- The report's `origin` and `destination` are the payload's airports, copied from the bundled list (iata, name, city, time zone, coordinates) (user confirmed assumption)
- `distanceKm` is the great-circle distance between the two airports (haversine, mean Earth radius 6371 km), rounded to whole kilometres (user confirmed assumption)
- `path` is the great-circle route sampled at a configured number of evenly spaced points (64 by default, including both airports), longitudes normalized to [-180, 180] (user confirmed assumption)
- `arrivalAt` = `departureAt` + `durationMin`; `durationMin` is the time the speed profile takes over the distance (climb and descent at their average speed, cruise at the model's cruise speed), rounded to whole minutes (user confirmed assumption)
- Each waypoint's `distanceFlownKm` grows from 0 at departure to `distanceKm` at arrival, and its `at` grows from `departureAt` to `arrivalAt` (user confirmed assumption)
- The handler logs the start and the generated flight (number, distance, duration) with job id, attempt and instance id; a simulated failure at warn level (user confirmed assumption)
- The handler lives in `infra/flight-transit/`; its generated-data tables (aircraft models, airline codes) are constants next to it in that module (user confirmed assumption)
- Tests: unit tests for the handler (one per spec scenario, `Random` and `Sleeper` mocked); no new per-app e2e or system e2e (user confirmed assumption)
- A flight shorter than the configured climb plus descent distance carries three waypoints: Departure, one "Peak" waypoint at the midpoint at the lower peak altitude, Arrival; no Top of climb, Cruise or Top of descent points (user)
- Time zones: `@date-fns/tz` (1.5.0, 2026-05-21, ~41.5M weekly downloads, own types, ESM) turns a local time on the departure date into a UTC instant; chosen over `temporal-polyfill` and `luxon` (user)
- Geo math: `@turf/distance` + `@turf/along` (7.4.0, 2026-08-03, ~6.3M / ~3.0M weekly downloads, own types, ESM) for the great-circle distance and points along the route; chosen over `@turf/great-circle` and `geolib` (user)

## Assumed

- [x] The report's `origin` and `destination` are the payload's airports, copied from the bundled list (iata, name, city, time zone, coordinates) - the report describes the submitted flight
- [x] `distanceKm` is the great-circle distance between the two airports (haversine, mean Earth radius 6371 km), rounded to whole kilometres - standard formula, readable numbers
- [x] `path` is the great-circle route sampled at a configured number of evenly spaced points (64 by default, including both airports), longitudes normalized to [-180, 180] - smooth line on the map, bounded size
- [x] `arrivalAt` = `departureAt` + `durationMin`; `durationMin` is the time the speed profile takes over the distance (climb and descent at their average speed, cruise at the model's cruise speed), rounded to whole minutes - consistent times
- [x] Each waypoint's `distanceFlownKm` grows from 0 at departure to `distanceKm` at arrival, and its `at` grows from `departureAt` to `arrivalAt` - the hover details stay consistent with the report
- [x] The handler logs the start and the generated flight (number, distance, duration) with job id, attempt and instance id - existing logging rule
- [x] The handler lives in `infra/flight-transit/`; its generated-data tables (aircraft models, airline codes) are constants next to it in that module - CLAUDE.md module layout
- [x] Tests: unit tests for the handler (one per spec scenario, `Random` and `Sleeper` mocked); no new per-app e2e or system e2e - same levels as `webhook-job-execution`
